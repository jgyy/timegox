# Ascension of the Azure Cloud: notes for AI contributors

## Art rule
**All art is generated from scripts.** Every model, texture, animation and effect must be reproducible by running code in this repository. Do not add hand-made, downloaded or AI-image-generated asset files. Screenshots in `docs/screenshots` are outputs, not inputs.

- **Godot code** (`scripts/`, GDScript and shaders): anything built from game data or changing at runtime, such as terrain, weather, spell effects, shaders and UI.
- **Blender Python** (`art/`, `bpy` for the latest Blender release): hero assets such as characters, monsters, rigs, animations and baked textures. Each script exports a `.glb` into `assets/` via `blender --background --python <script>`. Exported files are rebuilt from their scripts and never edited by hand.
- `art/lib/kit.py` is the shared toolkit (Godot-space coordinates, tint UV, toon/plain/glow material slots, baked AO, rigs, clips); `art/lib/person.py` and `art/lib/creature.py` build characters and monsters. `python art/build_all.py` (with `pip install bpy`) rebuilds everything; run `godot --headless --path . --import` afterwards.
- **Exports are packed.** `kit.export` runs `art/lib/pack.py` on every `.glb` it writes (byte weights, 16-bit tint UVs and bone rotations, no tracks for bones that rest, shared time tracks, compact JSON: about a third smaller, and Godot imports the same meshes and clips). Never commit a raw export; `python art/pack_all.py` packs anything exported with `ART_PACK=0`. Do not quantise normals or positions: Godot's importer refuses `KHR_mesh_quantization`.
- **A beast is not painted one bright colour all over.** `Assets.monster` takes the colour a foe was given down to what a hide can be for the shapes of flesh, fur, shell and scale (`Assets.NATURAL`, `Assets.natural`: less saturated, a little towards brown) and keeps the brightness for its markings and second colour; spirits, flames, jellies, birds of fire and dragons are as bright as written. A new shape of beast goes in `NATURAL`; a foe that must stay loud says `"bright": true`. Design a creature silhouette first (what is its highest point? what leaves the outline?), then value (dark back and points, pale belly and chest), and colour last and sparingly.
- **Animals that pull or carry are mounts** (`art/mounts/`, `Assets.MOUNTS`: the horse, the ox of the cart): sculpted and rigged like monsters, with idle, walk and run, and whatever they wear (saddle, yoke) modelled on them.
- After any art change, render and look at screenshots: `godot --path . --fixed-fps 30 -- --tour=<dir> --areas=<id> --no-extras --gallery` (use `xvfb-run` on a server).
- **Screenshots are WebP, never PNG.** Every capture the tour, the gallery or the bot writes is saved with `Image.save_webp(path, true, 0.9)` (lossy, quality 0.9: visually the same as the PNG, 4 to 10 times smaller). Name new captures `.webp` and link them from the README with `.webp`. Do not commit PNG screenshots; if one turns up, convert it with `magick in.png -quality 90 -define webp:method=6 out.webp` and delete the PNG.

## Trials (many roads)
- The bosses that carry the main story forward are guarded by **trials** in `scripts/trials.gd`: each has three or four ways through (might, craft, wealth, respect, Dao, friendship, relic), and only a met way can be taken. A new chapter's lords should get trials too, with recipes whose essences can be hunted on the way and quest items found before them (`tests/run_tests.gd` checks this).
- Give every new trial a planned way in the bot's `TRIAL_PLAN` (`scripts/bot.gd`) that it can actually work towards, and keep respect thresholds above the renown the bosses and trials alone give.

## Crossroads, works and the hours of the wild
- **Choices are crossroads** (`scripts/echoes.gd`): a question asked once after a step, whose answers tip one of three scales (mercy and rigour, new works and old ways, the wide world and the home fire), give something, **echo** in later conversations (`echo`: a dialogue key later in the story -> lines for each answer) and are told at the end (`end`). A new chapter should have at least one crossroads of its own, give each of its answers something to leave standing (`scripts/marks.gd`, below), give earlier ones an echo in its conversations, and give the bot an answer in `CROSS_PLAN` (`scripts/bot.gd`); `tests/run_tests.gd` checks every echo lands in a real, later conversation and fits the dialogue box. Conversations are fetched through `Echoes.told`, never straight from `Data.DIALOGUE`.
- **Technology is works** (`scripts/works.gd`, `scripts/machines.gd`): eighteen things to build in three schools (the Hand, the Wheel, the Art), paid for in know-how (learned by looking: bestiary, lords, things tried, obstacles, deeds, caves) and spirit stones. Each changes one number through `Works.mod` / `Works.add` and stands in the land as a machine built from code. A journey's know-how must never buy all eighteen (the test checks it), so that which to build stays a choice. Trials can be passed "By the works" (`["works", school or "" or "best", n]`).
- **Monsters keep hours** (`scripts/habits.gd`): every new enemy gets a habit from its shape and element (or an entry in `Habits.OWN`), and what a pack is doing at the hour it is met (asleep, hunting, resting, gathered) is drawn in the land and changes the battle. Bosses keep no hours.

## Caves, rooms and things to do
- **The ground is hollow** (`scripts/voxel.gd`, `scripts/caves.gd`): every region but the sect's own courtyard has a cave made of voxels (half a metre a side, four to a tile), laid out from the region's seed and entered by a cave mouth that `Caves.place` stands on open ground. A cave is a floor of its realm (`f:<area>:cave:-1`, a lower gallery `-2`), like a cellar: its own tiles, people and things. A new region gets one by itself (give its theme a look in `Caves.LOOKS` / `LOOK_OF`); a cave of the story gets an entry in `Caves.OWN` (name, size, where its mouth should stand, what lives in it, a lower gallery).
- **What the story puts in a cave is written with its region** in `Data.AREAS` like everybody else, with `"late": true, "cave": -1` (or `-2`) and `"stand": "deep" | "mid" | "near" | "any"`; `Caves.world` stands it on free ground. A step done in a cave carries `"cave": -1`; a hunt there cannot ask for the first strike (the sword's wake is for the open land). New step kinds: `"delve"` (go to the very back of n caves) and `"try"` (do n things of a cave, or the `things` named). The bot goes down by itself (`_goto_layer`, `_fetch`, `_delve_step`, `_try_step`). Trials can be passed "By the dark" (`["delved", n]`).
- **Only the lay of a cave (tiles, levels, roofs) is made when the world is built**; the voxels are made and meshed when somebody walks in (`Caves.volume`, `Voxel.mesh`, in pieces of six tiles so that the renderer's eight lights per mesh are enough). Keep it that way: the bot and the tests never need the rock.
- **Buildings are let out into the ground they have to spare** (`Rooms.let_out`, the last thing `MapGen.build` does): a tile or two on every side but the door's, where nothing stands, no other house is a tile away and everything that could be walked to still can be. Write a building's `rect` as the least it needs; `"fixed": true` keeps one as written, and a region's `"grow": 0` keeps all of its houses so. `rect0` is the rectangle as written (whose land is whose is counted by it). **A house may have one or two wings** (`wings`: a side room built out from a wall, `{rect, side}`; the wall is open along it, `Rooms.wing_gap`), drawn by `Arch._wing` with a gabled roof of its own; ask `Rooms.inside(bd, tile)`, never `bd.rect.has_point`, whether a tile is indoors. A style in `Rooms.NO_WINGS` stays a rectangle, and a crowded region caps them with `"wing_max"`. **Houses stand two to five storeys** (`Rooms.STOREYS` by style, or a building's own `"storeys"`): the ground floor and the one above are walked in, the rest are drawn (they lift away with the roof), and every storey has a use (`Rooms.USES`: most of a town is homes and offices; the tests count it). A new style needs a line in both.
- **Each people's town builds its own way** (`Arch.DRESS`, by country in `Arch.DRESS_OF`: colours blended over the building's style, a roof kind of the country's own such as `"flat"`, and what it hangs on a house: chimneys, a row of lanterns, a dome, the colour its windows are lit). A new town in a new country gets a dress; a style in `Arch.OWN_ROOF` keeps its roof anywhere. **People are found indoors** (`Rooms.residents`, placed last of all: one to three a house by the use of its ground floor, `Rooms.INDOORS`, in the bodies of the country, `Rooms.INDOOR_BODIES`, with the greeting of its hearth): a new use needs titles, bodies and lines there. **Somebody answers for every town** (`scripts/offices.gd`, `OFFICES`: a name and title, the building they sit in, a body, at least three lines; `Rooms.residents` seats them first, at the back of the room): a new town gets one, and the Son of Heaven and the Empress keep court in the Cauldron Hall of Tushan (he reigns, the hearths rule: nothing in the story may have him command anybody). **The trades of a town can be worked at** (`Offices.TRADES`, one for each use of a ground floor): a shift takes hours, pays by `Offices.wage`, once a day a town; "Not today" is always the first answer, no step may ask for a shift, and the bot never works one. **A trade is a career** (`Offices.RANKS`, `CAREERS`: five places a trade, by shifts worked; a rise pays more, the third place works an hour quicker, the fourth is on the books and draws a salary for up to five days away): a new trade needs its five names; keep the top of any career worth less than the road. **Water keeps two tiles off every wall** (`Rooms.dry`, `WATER_OFF`): do not write a pond against a house. What a house looks like from outside is `Arch.house`: the bays, windows and dado are `Arch._facade` (the upper storey uses it too), the flowers at the foot of the wall `Arch._border`; a new style needs only its colours in `Arch.STYLES`.
- **Every building has a cellar and, unless its roof takes none, an upper storey** (`scripts/rooms.gd`): floors written by hand in `Data.AREAS` stay as they are, the rest are laid out from the building's rectangle. A new building style needs an entry in `Rooms.UPPER` and `Rooms.CELLAR` (or in `NO_UPPER`); new furniture needs a kind in `Props.KINDS`, a line in `Props.FURNITURE` and a model in `Rooms.furnish` or `Architecture._furnish`.
- **A new thing to do** needs its clip in `art/lib/person.py` (`INTERACT`, so the party has it; add it to `Assets.LEND` if townsfolk are to borrow it), what is held in `Props.HELD` with a model, and three lines of its own. Gestures everybody has go in `person.CLIPS` and `Data.EMOTES`: that rebuilds every character.

## Mountains, climbing, the cloud-kite and the people of the road
- **The mountains are a term in the height field** (`scripts/peaks.gd`): `Peaks.place` gives every region peaks from its seed, on ground nothing stands on and wholly inside the region's own land (so no peak joins two regions and the seals keep what they kept), and `Peaks.rise` (a few sines, no mesh) is added in `Terrain3D.world_heights`. Everything that asks how high the ground is asks `Peaks.rise` / `Peaks.at`: the bot and the tests climb and glide without drawing anything. A region's peaks are numbered from its highest down (`peak:<area>:<k>`); a peak of the story gets its name in `Peaks.OWN`.
- **A step that rises more than `Peaks.STEEP` is climbed** (`World.climb`, the clip `scale`), and **V opens the cloud-kite** (`World.toggle_glide`, `scripts/glider.gd`, the clips `glide` and `alight`): a flight loses `GLIDE_SINK` metres a tile, never crosses into a region he has not walked into (`Game.seen`), a closed seal or the open sea, passes over a wood, a roof or anybody standing only with air to spare (`GLIDE_OVER`), and ends only where somebody can stand and walk away (`_landable`). Keep it so: gliding must never be a way round the story.
- **What the story puts on a peak is written with its region** in `Data.AREAS` like everybody else, with `"late": true, "peak": k` and `"stand": "top" | "flank" | "foot"`; `Peaks.world` stands it there (the very top tile is left for whoever climbs). New step kinds: `"summit"` (`"peak": area` and `"k"`, or `"n"` summits, any of them) and `"glide"` (one flight of `n` tiles). Trials can be passed "By the heights" (`["summits", n]`). The bot climbs and flies by itself (`_summit_step`, `_glide_step`).
- **The land between the towns is lived in** (`scripts/folk.gd`, `CAMP_ROLES`): a camp at the foot of the highest peak of every region that has room for one (`Folk.place_camps`), whose seven people (woodcutter, herbalist, angler, pedlar, hunter, pilgrim, hermit) are made, moved and talked to by everything that makes the people of the towns: a camp is a town with no houses (`Folk.town`, `Folk.homes_in`). A new kind of person of the road needs a day in `Folk.ROLES` that never goes `"in"`, places in `Folk.PLACES`, bodies, a title and at least four lines. An errand among them is a `"folk"` step whose `role` is one of theirs.
- A region may have **a second war-band for a later chapter** (`"warband2"` in `Data.AREAS`).

## Faces, arms and the ends of the story
- **Every human head has six bones of expression** that no clip moves (`art/lib/face.py`: `brow.L`, `brow.R`, `lip.L`, `lip.R`, `lids` over the eye bone, `jaw` over the mouth bone), with the brows and lips weighted to them wholly and the skin under them partly. **A face is a pose of those bones written in `scripts/faces.gd`** (`EXPRESSIONS`), worn over whatever clip plays: `Faces.wear(model, id, seconds)`, `Faces.keep(model, id)`. A new gesture or battle action gets a face in `Faces.OF_ACT`, a new thing to do in `Faces.OF_CLIP`; a line of dialogue may name one as a fourth element (`["Speaker", "text", "nod", "sorrow"]`), otherwise its words are read for one. Do not key the six bones in a clip, and do not write morph targets (`pack.py` refuses them).
- **The hero's weapon is one of the Eight Arms** (`scripts/arms.gd`, models in `art/lib/arms.py` exported by `art/weapons/<id>.py`, standing on the grip with their length along +Y). The hero's models carry a `sword` bone and no blade of their own (`"arms": True`); `Arms.attach` hangs the weapon there, so a clip that stows the sword stows any of them. **A new weapon needs** a model, an entry in `Arms.WEAPONS` (its Dao, its `mods` by rank, where it is won), a clip for its blow and one for its technique in `person.ARMS` (`arm_<id>`, `art_<id>`: held poses eased by `_keyed`), and a technique in `Data.SKILLS` with `"arm"`, `k` and `ck` (measured against what the hero already knows: `Game.skill_of`). Each weapon keeps its own mastery (`Game.arm_xp`); the tests check that no two have the same blow, technique or Dao.
- A hunt may ask for a weapon (`"arm": "<id>"`: only what falls while it is in the hand counts), a step may ask for Daos of arms (`"kind": "arms", "n": 3`), and trials can be passed "By the hand" (`["arms", n]`). The bot changes weapons by itself (`_want_arm`): give it nothing to do for a new one but own it.
- **The story has twelve ends** (`Cultures.ENDINGS`), and not all of them are kind: `kind` is `"bright"`, `"bitter"` or `"dark"`, and a bitter or dark end says so at the start of its `desc`, has `"hearth": false`, three lines of its own, a last word in `Ending.LAST_WORDS` and, if it is not an ascension, a heading for the results in `Ending.HEADLINES`. An end is open only to the journey that earned it (`needs`: hearths, peoples, Yue Ling, Daos of arms, works, the way a scale of the crossroads leans, or having sat at nobody's table). A new chapter's last crossroads should tip the scales the ends ask about.

## Music, war, the notice board, feats and the mat
- **There is no audio file in the repository**, and there must never be one: every tune is numbered notation in `Music.TUNES` (`scripts/music.gd`: `1 2 3 5 6` the five tones, `-` holds a beat, `_` halves a note, `'` and `,` are octaves, `|` a bar of four beats) with a key, a tempo and voices, and every instrument is arithmetic (`_tone`, `_pluck`, `_struck`, `_hit`). `Music.wanted` says what the screen, the place and the hour call for; a new kind of country needs its theme in `Music.OF_THEME`, a new screen with a scene of its own a line in `wanted`. A new tune goes in `ORDER` (the songbook) and the tests check its bars. With no window nothing is synthesised; what would be playing is still worked out (`Game.heard`).
- **Warfare is `scripts/war.gd` (rules, pure) and `scripts/field.gd` (the screen).** The host is companies of a hundred (`War.COMPANIES`: a `levy` from a hearth's keeper, a `step`, a `work` or a `job` brings each); a field battle is one order to a drum (`War.ORDERS`, what each does against each in `War.DEAL`) against a plan the scouts can read unless the dust hides it. Nothing is random: `War.step` is a function of the state and the order, and `War.best` is what the bot gives. A new field goes in `War.BATTLES` (a story field is a `"kind": "war"` step with `battle` and the `npc` who commands there); the tests fight every field with the levy, well led and badly, and it must be won the first way and lost the second. Trials can be passed "By the host" (`["companies", n]`) and "By the song" (`["airs", n]`).
- **Side quests are the notice board** (`scripts/jobs.gd`, Clerk Bai in Qingshi): a notice has one `need` (`have`, `kills`, `more`, `reach`, `folk`, `war`), an `after` step and what it pays. A step may ask that so many be done (`"kind": "jobs"`); never name one in particular.
- **Feats** (`scripts/feats.gd`) are comparisons (`test`: what, how much, read by `Feats.value`), told once when they become true; none may ask for more than there is (the tests count). **Finds** (`scripts/eggs.gd`) are not feats: no step, notice or hint in the story names one, and the page shows only half a hint until each is found. Do not document where they are in the README beyond saying that they exist.
- **Outfits** are earned by the story (`step`), a seller (`buy`, `at`), a feat, a commission or a find (`feat`, `job`, `egg`); Yue Ling has dresses of her own (`Fashion.YUE_OUTFITS`: `bond`, `step`, `buy`, `feat`). A new thing an outfit hangs on somebody needs a bone in `Fashion.ACCESSORY_BONES` and a model in `Models.accessory`; an outfit's words must read of a woman too (no "he" for anybody else in them).
- **Survival is optional and off by default** (`scripts/needs.gd`: `Game.survival`). Everything it does goes through `Needs.mod` (1 when off) and `Needs.used` / `Needs.tick` (no-ops when off): keep it so, and the bot's ordinary run and every older test stay as they were. A need is answered by things that already stand in the land (`BY_KIND`, `BY_EFFECT`) and by `Needs.actions`, which says why a thing cannot be done here; nobody may die of a need, and there must always be something to do about hunger and thirst in the wild (`forage`). The bot tends it with `--survival` (`Needs.advice`).
- **Courtship** (`scripts/romance.gd`) adds to bonds.gd and changes nothing in it: gifts, hours together (each `where` is a kind of place and hour read from `main.body_ctx()`), and three questions in order. No step, trial or ending may need any of it, the bot leaves it alone, and Yue Ling answers as herself (her `future` of Part XV is respected). Write its lines so they read of a hero of either sex: nobody calls the hero a man in them.
- **Gambling** (`scripts/gamble.gd`, Old Du's mat) draws every throw from `Game.gamble_n`, so the same journey throws the same dice; the house keeps an edge on every bet (the tests throw ten thousand rounds) and the bot walks away. Do not add a game the house can lose at over time.
- A quarter of a town that shares its region with another town is a town of its own in `Folk.TOWNS` with a `reach` (its people keep to its own streets); ground the packs must keep off because something is stood there after the map is made is listed in the region's `"clear"`.

## What the choices leave standing, and what cannot be beaten
- **Every answer of every crossroads stands somewhere in the land** (`scripts/marks.gd`, `MARKS`: crossroads -> answer -> `area`, `m` (a model built from code in `Marks.model`), `n`, `desc`, `k` if it is several things, `who` if it leaves somebody: a name, a body of the towns, a colour and at least two lines). A new crossroads needs an entry for each of its answers (the tests check it, and that it found room), and should change the look of its own country: a row of things (`k`) rather than one. All the answers of a crossroads share their ground in a region (`Marks.sites`); only the one chosen is ever built (`Marks.key`, `World._mark_model`), so a new model costs nothing until somebody chooses it.
- **A scale that leans stands a sign in every region** (`Marks.SIGNS`: one model for each way each of the three scales can lean). `Marks.place` runs last of all when a realm is made, on open ground with open ground all round it: keep it last, so that no older map moves, and never stand a mark where it could shut something in.
- **A doom is a foe that cannot be beaten** (`"doom": true` in `Data.ENEMIES`, an entry in `Dooms.DOOMS`: the round it crests on, what its last blow is called, and for the ones that roam what is said as a pack passes). The battle is fought in earnest and lost (`Battle._fall`, the outcome `"fall"`), or given way to (the fifth line of the battle menu); **nothing is taken for it**: no stones, no death counted, nobody carried home. Keep it so. A doom has no trial and no "post:" conversation: it has "pre:<id>", "fall:<id>" and "fall:<id>:struck" / ":kept" / ":gave" (how it was lost is remembered in `Game.falls` and tips a scale once).
- New step kinds: `"fall"` (`boss`: a doom of the story, gone from the land when the step is done: give its entry in `bosses` the step as the end of its `vis`), `"falls"` (`n` packs of the dooms named in `of`, which roam as a war-band of nothing else with an `"until"`), and `"marks"` (go and look at `n` things that stand; never name one). Trials can be passed "By the loss" (`["falls", n]`) and "By what stands" (`["marks", n]`). The bot fights a doom as it fights anything (`--yield`: gives way) and goes to look by itself (`_marks_step`).
- A region may have **a third and a fourth war-band** (`"warband3"`, `"warband4"`), and a band may stop marching when a step is done (`"until"`). They are placed after the caves, the peaks and the camps (`Atlas._place_bands`), so that nothing older moves for them: **a new chapter's bands should be these**, not `"warband"` or `"warband2"`, and what it stands in a region should be written on a peak or in a cave (`"peak"`, `"cave"`) rather than at a fixed tile. Compare every entity's place with the last release before pushing (dump `Atlas.world_of(layer).ents`, `peaks` and `camps` from both checkouts): only new things may differ.
- **The last lords are made of what was chosen** (`scripts/reckon.gd`): before the final breakthrough the step `s_p24_reckon` (kind `"reckon"`) stands on the mountain's peaks whoever the three scales made: the lord of the scale that leans furthest and of every other the hero is known for, or the Unchosen if none leans (`Reckon.due`). Each is as strong as the habit that made it (`Reckon.might`) and cracked by every time the other answer was given (`Reckon.cracks`). A new scale, or a new way one can lean, needs a lord in `Reckon.LORDS`, an enemy, a body, a place on a peak and its words (`WHAT`, `END`); the tests check every journey meets at least one and can beat it.
- **Carriages seat more than two** (`Fashion.RIDES` with `"kind": "carriage"` and `seats`: the hero's, Yue Ling's, then the passengers'; `seat` and `pillion` are the first two). They are bought (`buy`, `at`: an office of `Offices.OFFICES`), never given by a step, built in `Models._carriage` with wheels that turn (meta `wheels`), and everybody aboard sits (`World._carriage_view`, the clip `sit_bench`). **Fares** (`Offices.fare_offer`, `take_fare`, `arrive`): somebody at an inn, a tea-house or a shop asks to be carried to another town when a seat is free and pays on arrival; "Not today" stays the first answer, no step may ask for a fare, the bot buys no carriage and carries nobody, and a fare must stay worth less than the road.
- **The post** (`scripts/mail.gd`): whoever answers for a town keeps a counter (`Mail.POSTS`, one a region; the court keeps none). **Letters** (`LETTERS`, in `ORDER`) come after a step (`after`, and `wait` steps for the carrier), most of them only to the journey that gave an answer at a crossroads (`needs`: crossroads, answer; then `after` is that crossroads' step), may enclose something (`gift`: `stones` or `item`, `n`), and may be answered two ways (`reply`), each answer bringing a later letter (`on`: letter, answer). **A new crossroads should have a letter for at least one of its answers**, written by somebody who lives with what was chosen. Every line must fit the dialogue box and is spoken by its sender, so it reads of a hero of either sex: call the hero "Lin Feng", "you" or "child". **Post-bags** (`bag_offer`, `take_bag`, `deliver`) go between counters of one realm, one a day a counter, three at most. No step, trial, notice or ending may ask for a letter or a bag, nothing in the post tips a scale, "Not today" stays first at the counter, and the bot leaves it alone.
- **The towns are joined by a road, and nothing moves for it** (`scripts/roads.gd`): `Roads.of(layer)` works out, the first time it is asked and never when the world is built, a high road between the towns of a realm (`Data.TOWNS`) and a lane off it to where travellers arrive in every other region, over open ground and the regions' own paths, round peaks, houses and whatever stands. It is **not written into the grid**: it is a set of tiles beside the realm, paved as each region is drawn (`Roads.mesh`, from `World._build_view`), drawn on the map page, and asked by `Roads.pace` (a carriage is quicker on a road or a path and slower off one; a horse, a sword and a cloud go as they always did, so the bot's journey is the same). A new town is on the road by being in `Data.TOWNS`. **The post-coach** (`coach_offers`, `ride_coach`, at a counter of the post) carries the hero for a fare along the high road to a town of a region already walked into, never past a closed seal: keep it so, it must not be a way round the story.

## Hearths (the six cultures) and who the hero is
- The peoples of the land are the **Six Hearths** in `scripts/cultures.gd` (towns, keepers, greetings, tables, fires, customs, standing, the six beginnings and the five endings). A new town of the land belongs to a hearth (`towns`, `areas`); a new chapter's errands for a hearth carry `"hearth": "<id>"` on their steps, done in that hearth's own way, and an errand among its townsfolk names its `"town"`.
- The hero can be a man or a woman. **Write story text about "Lin Feng" and "he" as always**; `scripts/voice.gd` tells it of her on the way to the screen (narration by rule, speech only by what the hero is called). Where the rule gets a new line wrong, add the whole line to `Voice.FIX`; `tests/run_tests.gd` reads every conversation through it. Words about nobody but the hero (an outfit, a ride) go through `Voice.her`.
- New people need a Blender script in `art/characters/`; women are marked `"fem": True` there and, if they are named without a woman's title, listed in `Voice.WOMEN`.

## Performance
- `godot --path . -- --bench=build/bench.json` (with `xvfb-run` on a server) times the opening of the world and counts what a frame draws; `--census` lists the triangles in the scene by kind (`--census-view`: only what the camera sees). Compare draw calls and primitives between two builds on the same machine; the frame rate of a software renderer says little.
