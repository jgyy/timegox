# Ascension of the Azure Cloud: notes for AI contributors

## Art rule
**All art is generated from scripts.** Every model, texture, animation and effect must be reproducible by running code in this repository. Do not add hand-made, downloaded or AI-image-generated asset files. Screenshots in `docs/screenshots` are outputs, not inputs.

- **Godot code** (`scripts/`, GDScript and shaders): anything built from game data or changing at runtime, such as terrain, weather, spell effects, shaders and UI.
- **Blender Python** (`art/`, `bpy` for the latest Blender release): hero assets such as characters, monsters, rigs, animations and baked textures. Each script exports a `.glb` into `assets/` via `blender --background --python <script>`. Exported files are rebuilt from their scripts and never edited by hand.
- `art/lib/kit.py` is the shared toolkit (Godot-space coordinates, tint UV, toon/plain/glow material slots, baked AO, rigs, clips); `art/lib/person.py` and `art/lib/creature.py` build characters and monsters. `python art/build_all.py` (with `pip install bpy`) rebuilds everything; run `godot --headless --path . --import` afterwards.
- **Exports are packed.** `kit.export` runs `art/lib/pack.py` on every `.glb` it writes (byte weights, 16-bit tint UVs and bone rotations, no tracks for bones that rest, shared time tracks, compact JSON: about a third smaller, and Godot imports the same meshes and clips). Never commit a raw export; `python art/pack_all.py` packs anything exported with `ART_PACK=0`. Do not quantise normals or positions: Godot's importer refuses `KHR_mesh_quantization`.
- **A beast is not painted one bright colour all over.** `Assets.monster` takes the colour a foe was given down to what a hide can be for the shapes of flesh, fur, shell and scale (`Assets.NATURAL`, `Assets.natural`: less saturated, a little towards brown) and keeps the brightness for its markings and second colour; spirits, flames, jellies, birds of fire and dragons are as bright as written. A new shape of beast goes in `NATURAL`; a foe that must stay loud says `"bright": true`. Design a creature silhouette first (what is its highest point? what leaves the outline?), then value (dark back and points, pale belly and chest), and colour last and sparingly.
- **Animals that pull or carry are mounts** (`art/mounts/`, `Assets.MOUNTS`: the horse, the ox of the cart): sculpted and rigged like monsters, with idle, walk and run, and whatever they wear (saddle, yoke) modelled on them.
- **Every person has a body under the clothes** (`art/lib/body.py`, built by `person.py` for every script in `art/characters/`): a character's `.glb` carries three meshes on one skeleton, `own` (head, hair, hands and what belongs to them), `dress` (the clothes and the neck) and `bare` (the whole figure in hemp underclothes: a waist-wrap and, for a woman, a breast-band, tied with knots, hidden by default). The figure is sculpted from measured proportions (a head about an eighth of the height, shoulders, chest, waist and hip by sex, age, build and muscle in `Figure`), with every mass tagged so that the seams between muscles stay soft. `Models.undress(m)` swaps `dress` for `bare` and hides what is hung on the bones; the wardrobe has "Down to the underclothes", and a soak in a hot spring undresses for its length. A new character needs nothing for it; keep a person under the triangle budget of `art/check_assets.py` (the `bare` mesh is decimated to `person.BARE_TRIS`). Heads are drawn at life size (`person.HEAD_K`), so what is hung on a head is scaled by `Models.head_k`.
- **The body is measured, and it has the lines a body has** (the Broken Mirror release). `art/lib/body.py` was checked against a reference figure of each sex (the CC0 MakeHuman base mesh with its young man and young woman, laid over the rig joint for joint and cut into the same slices; the numbers are in the README). **A reference model is something to measure, never something to commit**: it is fetched to a scratch directory, sliced, and deleted. What melted clay cannot hold is cut afterwards by `body._carve` (a displacement and a shade, in metres): the cleft between the two cheeks and the fold under each, the sternum between two breasts or two pectorals and the lower border of each, the furrow of the spine, the linea alba, the navel. Cloth lies across a cleft (`CLOTH_BRIDGE`), so the same forms show through the underclothes, shallower. A new form of the body goes in `_carve` with its depth from a reference, not as a thinner piece of clay: a capsule thinner than the clay's voxel tears the skin into a pit (the collarbones did). Everybody stays in their underclothes.
- **Monsters are built by kind** (this release's re-sculpt): `bird.py` (layered feathers, a bone per flight feather), `elemental.py` (stone, bark, cloud, flame), `arthropod.py` and `serpentine.py` (jointed shells, scaled bodies), `mammal.py` (fur, a jaw fitted under the lip). Read what the creature is first (anatomy, field guides, the Shanhaijing), and end every death clip lying on the ground, not under it: the game holds the last frame. **Do not tag a large body `SCALE`**: the toon shader draws a world-aligned grid of plates on that class, and it reads as a tablecloth; carve the scales and use a plain class. **A bird foe is drawn on the bird nearest its kind** (`Assets.BIRD_OF`: the waders on the crane, the crows and small birds on the magpie, the rest on the raptor-headed bird), and keeps the `"bird"` shape and its habits; `Assets.hovers` says who is lifted off the ground in the land.
- **A beast that is also a mount is the mount's script with its harness off** (`art/monsters/ox.py` runs `art/mounts/ox.py` with `OX_WILD`): the mount's export must stay byte-identical.
- After any art change, render and look at screenshots: `godot --path . --fixed-fps 30 -- --tour=<dir> --areas=<id> --no-extras --gallery` (use `xvfb-run` on a server).
- **Screenshots are WebP, never PNG.** Every capture the tour, the gallery or the bot writes is saved with `Image.save_webp(path, true, 0.9)` (lossy, quality 0.9: visually the same as the PNG, 4 to 10 times smaller). Name new captures `.webp` and link them from the README with `.webp`. Do not commit PNG screenshots; if one turns up, convert it with `magick in.png -quality 90 -define webp:method=6 out.webp` and delete the PNG.

## Trials (many roads)
- The bosses that carry the main story forward are guarded by **trials** in `scripts/trials.gd`: each has three or four ways through (might, craft, wealth, respect, Dao, friendship, relic), and only a met way can be taken. A new chapter's lords should get trials too, with recipes whose essences can be hunted on the way and quest items found before them (`tests/run_tests.gd` checks this).
- Give every new trial a planned way in the bot's `TRIAL_PLAN` (`scripts/bot.gd`) that it can actually work towards, and keep respect thresholds above the renown the bosses and trials alone give.

## Crossroads, works and the hours of the wild
- **Choices are crossroads** (`scripts/echoes.gd`): a question asked once after a step, whose answers tip one of three scales (mercy and rigour, new works and old ways, the wide world and the home fire), give something, **echo** in later conversations (`echo`: a dialogue key later in the story -> lines for each answer) and are told at the end (`end`). A new chapter should have at least one crossroads of its own, give each of its answers something to leave standing (`scripts/marks.gd`, below), give earlier ones an echo in its conversations, and give the bot an answer in `CROSS_PLAN` (`scripts/bot.gd`); `tests/run_tests.gd` checks every echo lands in a real, later conversation and fits the dialogue box. Conversations are fetched through `Echoes.told`, never straight from `Data.DIALOGUE`.
- **Technology is works** (`scripts/works.gd`, `scripts/machines.gd`): eighteen things to build in three schools (the Hand, the Wheel, the Art), paid for in know-how (learned by looking: bestiary, lords, things tried, obstacles, deeds, caves) and spirit stones. Each changes one number through `Works.mod` / `Works.add` and stands in the land as a machine built from code. A journey's know-how must never buy all eighteen (the test checks it), so that which to build stays a choice. Trials can be passed "By the works" (`["works", school or "" or "best", n]`).
- **Monsters keep hours** (`scripts/habits.gd`): every new enemy gets a habit from its shape and element (or an entry in `Habits.OWN`), and what a pack is doing at the hour it is met (asleep, hunting, resting, gathered) is drawn in the land and changes the battle. Bosses keep no hours.

## Caves, rooms and things to do
- **The ground is hollow** (`scripts/voxel.gd`, `scripts/caves.gd`): every region but the sect's own courtyard has a cave made of voxels (half a metre a side, four to a tile), laid out from the region's seed and entered by a cave mouth that `Caves.place` stands on open ground. A cave is a floor of its realm (`f:<area>:cave:-1`, a lower gallery `-2`), like a cellar: its own tiles, people and things. A new region gets one by itself (give its theme a look in `Caves.LOOKS` / `LOOK_OF`); a cave of the story gets an entry in `Caves.OWN` (name, size, where its mouth should stand, what lives in it, a lower gallery).
- **What the story puts in a cave is written with its region** in `Data.AREAS` like everybody else, with `"late": true, "cave": -1` (or `-2`) and `"stand": "deep" | "mid" | "near" | "any"`; `Caves.world` stands it on free ground. A step done in a cave carries `"cave": -1`; a hunt there cannot ask for the first strike (the sword's wake is for the open land). New step kinds: `"delve"` (go to the very back of n caves) and `"try"` (do n things of a cave, or the `things` named). The bot goes down by itself (`_goto_layer`, `_fetch`, `_delve_step`, `_try_step`). Trials can be passed "By the dark" (`["delved", n]`).
- **Only the lay of a cave (tiles, levels, roofs) is made when the world is built**; the voxels are made and meshed when somebody walks in (`Caves.volume`, `Voxel.mesh`, in pieces of six tiles so that the renderer's eight lights per mesh are enough). Keep it that way: the bot and the tests never need the rock.
- **Buildings are let out into the ground they have to spare** (`Rooms.let_out`, the last thing `MapGen.build` does): a tile or two on every side but the door's, where nothing stands, no other house is a tile away and everything that could be walked to still can be. Write a building's `rect` as the least it needs; `"fixed": true` keeps one as written, and a region's `"grow": 0` keeps all of its houses so. `rect0` is the rectangle as written (whose land is whose is counted by it). **A house may have one or two wings** (`wings`: a side room built out from a wall, `{rect, side}`; the wall is open along it, `Rooms.wing_gap`), drawn by `Arch._wing` with a gabled roof of its own; ask `Rooms.inside(bd, tile)`, never `bd.rect.has_point`, whether a tile is indoors. A style in `Rooms.NO_WINGS` stays a rectangle, and a crowded region caps them with `"wing_max"`. **Houses stand two to five storeys** (`Rooms.STOREYS` by style, or a building's own `"storeys"`): the ground floor and the one above are walked in, the rest are drawn (they lift away with the roof), and every storey has a use (`Rooms.USES`: most of a town is homes and offices; the tests count it). A new style needs a line in both.
- **Each people's town builds its own way** (`Arch.DRESS`, by country in `Arch.DRESS_OF`: colours blended over the building's style, a roof kind of the country's own such as `"flat"`, and what it hangs on a house: chimneys, a row of lanterns, a dome, the colour its windows are lit). A new town in a new country gets a dress; a style in `Arch.OWN_ROOF` keeps its roof anywhere. **People are found indoors** (`Rooms.residents`, placed last of all: one to three a house by the use of its ground floor, `Rooms.INDOORS`, in the bodies of the country, `Rooms.INDOOR_BODIES`, with the greeting of its hearth): a new use needs titles, bodies and lines there. **Somebody answers for every town** (`scripts/offices.gd`, `OFFICES`: a name and title, the building they sit in, a body, at least three lines; `Rooms.residents` seats them first, at the back of the room): a new town gets one, and the Son of Heaven and the Empress keep court in the Cauldron Hall of Tushan (he reigns, the hearths rule: nothing in the story may have him command anybody). **The trades of a town can be worked at** (`Offices.TRADES`, one for each use of a ground floor): a shift takes hours, pays by `Offices.wage`, once a day a town; "Not today" is always the first answer, no step may ask for a shift, and the bot never works one. **A trade is a career** (`Offices.RANKS`, `CAREERS`: five places a trade, by shifts worked; a rise pays more, the third place works an hour quicker, the fourth is on the books and draws a salary for up to five days away): a new trade needs its five names; keep the top of any career worth less than the road. **Water keeps two tiles off every wall** (`Rooms.dry`, `WATER_OFF`): do not write a pond against a house. **A window of a storey that is walked in is a hole in the wall** (`Arch._wall` leaves it, `Arch._window_bays` says where, `_facade(..., open = true)` frames it): the room is seen through it, so do not put a pane, a lattice or a glow across it; the storeys that are only drawn keep their lit lattices. What a house looks like from outside is `Arch.house`: the bays, windows and dado are `Arch._facade` (the upper storey uses it too), the flowers at the foot of the wall `Arch._border`; a new style needs only its colours in `Arch.STYLES`.
- **Every building has a cellar and, unless its roof takes none, an upper storey** (`scripts/rooms.gd`): floors written by hand in `Data.AREAS` stay as they are, the rest are laid out from the building's rectangle. A new building style needs an entry in `Rooms.UPPER` and `Rooms.CELLAR` (or in `NO_UPPER`); new furniture needs a kind in `Props.KINDS`, a line in `Props.FURNITURE` and a model in `Rooms.furnish` or `Architecture._furnish`.
- **A new thing to do** needs its clip in `art/lib/person.py` (`INTERACT`, so the party has it; add it to `Assets.LEND` if townsfolk are to borrow it), what is held in `Props.HELD` with a model, and three lines of its own. Gestures everybody has go in `person.CLIPS` and `Data.EMOTES`: that rebuilds every character.

## Mountains, climbing, the cloud-kite and the people of the road
- **The mountains are a term in the height field** (`scripts/peaks.gd`): `Peaks.place` gives every region peaks from its seed, on ground nothing stands on and wholly inside the region's own land (so no peak joins two regions and the seals keep what they kept), and `Peaks.rise` (a few sines, no mesh) is added in `Terrain3D.world_heights`. Everything that asks how high the ground is asks `Peaks.rise` / `Peaks.at`: the bot and the tests climb and glide without drawing anything. A region's peaks are numbered from its highest down (`peak:<area>:<k>`); a peak of the story gets its name in `Peaks.OWN`.
- **A step that rises more than `Peaks.STEEP` is climbed** (`World.climb`, the clip `scale`), and **V opens the cloud-kite** (`World.toggle_glide`, `scripts/glider.gd`, the clips `glide` and `alight`): a flight loses `GLIDE_SINK` metres a tile, never crosses into a region he has not walked into (`Game.seen`), a closed seal or the open sea, passes over a wood, a roof or anybody standing only with air to spare (`GLIDE_OVER`), and ends only where somebody can stand and walk away (`_landable`). Keep it so: gliding must never be a way round the story.
- **What the story puts on a peak is written with its region** in `Data.AREAS` like everybody else, with `"late": true, "peak": k` and `"stand": "top" | "flank" | "foot"`; `Peaks.world` stands it there (the very top tile is left for whoever climbs): people, lords, things to find, and things to do (`"props"`: `{"id", "prop", "peak", "stand"}`, the hot spring of Part XXVI; a region's own things to do are drawn by lot and may not include the one a step needs). New step kinds: `"summit"` (`"peak": area` and `"k"`, or `"n"` summits, any of them) and `"glide"` (one flight of `n` tiles). Trials can be passed "By the heights" (`["summits", n]`). The bot climbs and flies by itself (`_summit_step`, `_glide_step`).
- **The land between the towns is lived in** (`scripts/folk.gd`, `CAMP_ROLES`): a camp at the foot of the highest peak of every region that has room for one (`Folk.place_camps`), whose seven people (woodcutter, herbalist, angler, pedlar, hunter, pilgrim, hermit) are made, moved and talked to by everything that makes the people of the towns: a camp is a town with no houses (`Folk.town`, `Folk.homes_in`). A new kind of person of the road needs a day in `Folk.ROLES` that never goes `"in"`, places in `Folk.PLACES`, bodies, a title and at least four lines. An errand among them is a `"folk"` step whose `role` is one of theirs.
- A region may have **a second war-band for a later chapter** (`"warband2"` in `Data.AREAS`).

## Faces, arms and the ends of the story
- **Every human head has six bones of expression** that no clip moves (`art/lib/face.py`: `brow.L`, `brow.R`, `lip.L`, `lip.R`, `lids` over the eye bone, `jaw` over the mouth bone), with the brows and lips weighted to them wholly and the skin under them partly. **A face is a pose of those bones written in `scripts/faces.gd`** (`EXPRESSIONS`), worn over whatever clip plays: `Faces.wear(model, id, seconds)`, `Faces.keep(model, id)`. A new gesture or battle action gets a face in `Faces.OF_ACT`, a new thing to do in `Faces.OF_CLIP`; a line of dialogue may name one as a fourth element (`["Speaker", "text", "nod", "sorrow"]`), otherwise its words are read for one. Do not key the six bones in a clip, and do not write morph targets (`pack.py` refuses them).
- **The hero's weapon is one of the Eight Arms** (`scripts/arms.gd`, models in `art/lib/arms.py` exported by `art/weapons/<id>.py`, standing on the grip with their length along +Y). The hero's models carry a `sword` bone and no blade of their own (`"arms": True`); `Arms.attach` hangs the weapon there, so a clip that stows the sword stows any of them. **A new weapon needs** a model, an entry in `Arms.WEAPONS` (its Dao, its `mods` by rank, where it is won), a clip for its blow and one for its technique in `person.ARMS` (`arm_<id>`, `art_<id>`: held poses eased by `_keyed`), and a technique in `Data.SKILLS` with `"arm"`, `k` and `ck` (measured against what the hero already knows: `Game.skill_of`). Each weapon keeps its own mastery (`Game.arm_xp`); the tests check that no two have the same blow, technique or Dao.
- A hunt may ask for a weapon (`"arm": "<id>"`: only what falls while it is in the hand counts), a step may ask for Daos of arms (`"kind": "arms", "n": 3`), and trials can be passed "By the hand" (`["arms", n]`). The bot changes weapons by itself (`_want_arm`): give it nothing to do for a new one but own it.
- **The story has sixteen ends** (`Cultures.ENDINGS`), and not all of them are kind: `kind` is `"bright"`, `"bitter"` or `"dark"`, and a bitter or dark end says so at the start of its `desc`, has `"hearth": false`, three lines of its own, a last word in `Ending.LAST_WORDS` and, if it is not an ascension, a heading for the results in `Ending.HEADLINES`. An end is open only to the journey that earned it (`needs`: hearths, peoples, Yue Ling, Daos of arms, works, the way a scale of the crossroads leans, having sat at nobody's table, what the hero's own town has become (`["hold_rank", r]`) or what was answered at one crossroads (`["chose", crossroads, [answers]]`)). A new chapter's last crossroads should tip the scales the ends ask about.

## Music, war, the notice board, feats and the mat
- **There is no audio file in the repository**, and there must never be one: every tune is numbered notation in `Music.TUNES` (`scripts/music.gd`: `1 2 3 5 6` the five tones, `-` holds a beat, `_` halves a note, `'` and `,` are octaves, `|` a bar of four beats) with a key, a tempo and voices, and every instrument is arithmetic (`_tone`, `_pluck`, `_struck`, `_hit`). `Music.wanted` says what the screen, the place and the hour call for; a new kind of country needs its theme in `Music.OF_THEME`, a new screen with a scene of its own a line in `wanted`. A new tune goes in `ORDER` (the songbook) and the tests check its bars. With no window nothing is synthesised; what would be playing is still worked out (`Game.heard`).
- **Warfare is `scripts/war.gd` (rules, pure) and `scripts/field.gd` (the screen).** The host is companies of a hundred (`War.COMPANIES`: a `levy` from a hearth's keeper, a `step`, a `work` or a `job` brings each); a field battle is one order to a drum (`War.ORDERS`, what each does against each in `War.DEAL`) against a plan the scouts can read unless the dust hides it. Nothing is random: `War.step` is a function of the state and the order, and `War.best` is what the bot gives. A new field goes in `War.BATTLES` (a story field is a `"kind": "war"` step with `battle` and the `npc` who commands there); the tests fight every field with the levy, well led and badly, and it must be won the first way and lost the second. Trials can be passed "By the host" (`["companies", n]`) and "By the song" (`["airs", n]`).
- **Side quests are the notice board** (`scripts/jobs.gd`, Clerk Bai in Qingshi): a notice has one `need` (`have`, `kills`, `more`, `reach`, `folk`, `war`), an `after` step and what it pays. A step may ask that so many be done (`"kind": "jobs"`); never name one in particular.
- **Feats** (`scripts/feats.gd`) are comparisons (`test`: what, how much, read by `Feats.value`), told once when they become true; none may ask for more than there is (the tests count). **Finds** (`scripts/eggs.gd`) are not feats: no step, notice or hint in the story names one, and the page shows only half a hint until each is found. Do not document where they are in the README beyond saying that they exist.
- **Outfits** are earned by the story (`step`), a seller (`buy`, `at`), a feat, a commission or a find (`feat`, `job`, `egg`); Yue Ling has dresses of her own (`Fashion.YUE_OUTFITS`: `bond`, `step`, `buy`, `feat`). A new thing an outfit hangs on somebody needs a bone in `Fashion.ACCESSORY_BONES` and a model in `Models.accessory`; an outfit's words must read of a woman too (no "he" for anybody else in them).
- **Survival is optional and off by default** (`scripts/needs.gd`: `Game.survival`). Everything it does goes through `Needs.mod` (1 when off) and `Needs.used` / `Needs.tick` (no-ops when off): keep it so, and the bot's ordinary run and every older test stay as they were. A need is answered by things that already stand in the land (`BY_KIND`, `BY_EFFECT`) and by `Needs.actions`, which says why a thing cannot be done here; nobody may die of a need, and there must always be something to do about hunger and thirst in the wild (`forage`). The bot tends it with `--survival` (`Needs.advice`).
- **Courtship** (`scripts/romance.gd`) adds to bonds.gd and changes nothing in it: gifts, hours together (each `where` is a kind of place and hour read from `main.body_ctx()`), and three questions in order. Since the Broken Mirror release there is also what comes after a question: **touch** (`TOUCH`: three clips of `person.COURT` that both of them play face to face, `World.touch`; a new one needs a clip there, in `check_assets.PARTY_CLIPS`, a stage, and a line for every tone), **feast days together** (`FEASTS`, keyed by a name in `Almanac.FESTIVALS`, each once) and **what is the matter** (`MATTERS`: something the journey has done that she minds, read by `matter_true` from the state alone; said once when asked, made up only when the thing itself is put right, and not at all if it cannot be). While something is the matter and unsaid, nothing further is asked or answered: that may shut courtship, and must never shut anything else. No step, trial or ending may need any of it, the bot leaves it alone, and Yue Ling answers as herself (her `future` of Part XV is respected). Write its lines so they read of a hero of either sex: nobody calls the hero a man in them.
- **Gambling** (`scripts/gamble.gd`, Old Du's mat) draws every throw from `Game.gamble_n`, so the same journey throws the same dice; the house keeps an edge on every bet (the tests throw ten thousand rounds) and the bot walks away. Do not add a game the house can lose at over time.
- A quarter of a town that shares its region with another town is a town of its own in `Folk.TOWNS` with a `reach` (its people keep to its own streets); ground the packs must keep off because something is stood there after the map is made is listed in the region's `"clear"`.

## What the choices leave standing, and what cannot be beaten
- **Every answer of every crossroads stands somewhere in the land** (`scripts/marks.gd`, `MARKS`: crossroads -> answer -> `area`, `m` (a model built from code in `Marks.model`), `n`, `desc`, `k` if it is several things, `who` if it leaves somebody: a name, a body of the towns, a colour and at least two lines). A new crossroads needs an entry for each of its answers (the tests check it, and that it found room), and should change the look of its own country: a row of things (`k`) rather than one. All the answers of a crossroads share their ground in a region (`Marks.sites`); only the one chosen is ever built (`Marks.key`, `World._mark_model`), so a new model costs nothing until somebody chooses it.
- **A scale that leans stands a sign in every region** (`Marks.SIGNS`: one model for each way each of the three scales can lean). `Marks.place` runs last of all when a realm is made, on open ground with open ground all round it: keep it last, so that no older map moves, and never stand a mark where it could shut something in.
- **A doom is a foe that cannot be beaten** (`"doom": true` in `Data.ENEMIES`, an entry in `Dooms.DOOMS`: the round it crests on, what its last blow is called, and for the ones that roam what is said as a pack passes). The battle is fought in earnest and lost (`Battle._fall`, the outcome `"fall"`), or given way to (the fifth line of the battle menu); **nothing is taken for it**: no stones, no death counted, nobody carried home. Keep it so. A doom has no trial and no "post:" conversation: it has "pre:<id>", "fall:<id>" and "fall:<id>:struck" / ":kept" / ":gave" (how it was lost is remembered in `Game.falls` and tips a scale once).
- New step kinds: `"fall"` (`boss`: a doom of the story, gone from the land when the step is done: give its entry in `bosses` the step as the end of its `vis`), `"falls"` (`n` packs of the dooms named in `of`, which roam as a war-band of nothing else with an `"until"`), and `"marks"` (go and look at `n` things that stand; never name one; a later chapter's says `"new": true`, so that only what is looked at after it begins counts: a journey has looked at as many as `n` by then, and the step would be done the moment it was given). Trials can be passed "By the loss" (`["falls", n]`) and "By what stands" (`["marks", n]`). The bot fights a doom as it fights anything (`--yield`: gives way) and goes to look by itself (`_marks_step`).
- A region may have **a third and a fourth war-band** (`"warband3"`, `"warband4"`), and a band may stop marching when a step is done (`"until"`). They are placed after the caves, the peaks and the camps (`Atlas._place_bands`), and **a fifth and a sixth** (`"warband5"`, `"warband6"`) after the marks too, so that nothing older moves for them: a band placed before the marks takes ground a mark of an older release stood on, so **a new chapter's bands take the last slots** (`"warband5"`, `"warband6"`; if a region has both, add a seventh after them in `Atlas`, never before), and what it stands in a region should be written on a peak or in a cave (`"peak"`, `"cave"`) rather than at a fixed tile. **Never write a late-comer at a fixed tile** (`"late": true` with a `pos` and neither `"peak"` nor `"cave"`): those are stood before the peaks are laid out, and every peak, camp and mark of the region moves for them. ## What things weigh, the work of the hands, and the ring
- **Everything the hero has is on his back, in jin** (`scripts/load.gd`): `Load.weight_of` (**only bulk weighs**: the goods of the land, tools, food, and what a `collect` step is looking for, about a jin or what `WEIGHTS` says, until the step is done; a pill, an essence, a keepsake, a token and a thing made for a trial weigh nothing. ## How the towns stand, the work of the towns, and things that share ground
- **Every town is weighed** (`scripts/weal.gd`): eleven towns (`Weal.TOWNS`: a town, the regions whose news is its own, and the office that answers for it), each with three measures made of nothing but the journey: **plenty**, **peace** and **regard**. Lords put down, packs cleared, fields of the war, dooms given way to, works built, shifts, fares, the post, errands, things broken and gifts to the chest are the rest.

Trials can be passed "By the towns" (`["weal", n]`: so many towns thriving or festive). ## The day of the year, the Way he walks, and where things go
- **The days have names, and things happen in them that are not his doing** (`scripts/almanac.gd`): the date is `Game.day` plus `Game.cal`, an offset the story moves **forward only** when a chapter is set on a day of the year (`Almanac.STORY_DATES`, beside `Clock.STORY_HOURS`: a chapter set at a festival gets a line there). A battle is fought with the grown group (`Main.start_encounter` passes it as `ctx.group`, and what fell is counted from it), and every place a pack is beaten or scattered calls `Almanac.put_down`. **Lords keep no calendar**, nothing grows past `Data.MAX_LEVEL`, and the group the land wrote is never changed (the tests check all three).

The bot takes no Demonic art and never needs a side.

The first time a piece of a lived-in house is used, whoever lives there says what they think of it (`Fitting.touched`).

- **A country hunted too hard changes** (`scripts/wilds.gd`): every one of the land's own packs put down (`Wilds.native`: not a war-band, not of a cave; `Wilds.culled`, beside every `Almanac.put_down`) raises the region's strain, which eases by half every `HALF` days. A hunter of a camp can be paid to open three sides of his net (`open_net`). **A war-band of the story fights as it was written in any country**, and no hunt the story asks for may thin a country by itself (the tests count). A new state needs a line in every table.
- **Trials can be passed "By a clean name"** (`["clean"]`: nobody dead by his hand, no bill, the shelf never opened) **and "By the standing wild"** (`["wild"]`: nothing he has hunted in worse than thinned), and never only so: every trial keeps two ways open to somebody with a price on his head (the tests check). `["step", id]` is a need met by something of the story already done.

## The Great People, the face he has earned, and close-ups
- **There is a people twice a man's height** (`scripts/giants.gd`): the Daren, the tenth people of the realms (`Data.RACES.daren`). - **A better is somebody several realms above him, and losing to one is not the infirmary** (`scripts/betters.gd`, `"better": true` in `Data.ENEMIES`, an entry in `Betters.BETTERS` and `COST`): met at his own level on the day and `over` whole realms (at least four), with a lord's health and blow multiplied again for every realm of the gap past the first (`Betters.might`: a level is a few parts in a hundred in this land, and the weight of a realm stops at three, so without it somebody four realms up is a lord like any other, and the bot beat all three). The battle is fought in earnest; when the party is down, or he gives way (the fifth line of the menu), the outcome is `"bested"` (`Battle._bested`, `Main._better_done`): **nobody is carried home and no death is counted, and something is taken**: a share of his stones, days of his life (days lived: the land does not wait), the peace of the town that hears of that country (`Weal.ACTS.bested`), and a wound on a number (`Betters.mod`: asked in `Game.fighter` and `Weal.mod`) until the step that mends it is done. Nothing a wound weighs on may be something a step needs. A doom (dooms.gd) is the other thing: it cannot be beaten and nothing is taken for it. Keep both so.
- **He can be watched first, and met again** (`Betters.watch`: Enter in front of him, "Walk on" the first answer; four hours, once a day, three times, a realm of the gap closed each time; `Betters.again`: whoever lost may ask for another bout, and winning gives back the stones and lifts the wound but never the branch). A new better needs a body in `art/characters/`, a `person:` shape in `UI.SHAPES`, three things seen when watched, a story in `Lore.BOSS`, and words under `dare:`, `won:`, `bested:<id>:lost`, `bested:<id>:gave`, `again:` and `back:`. One who is `bow`ed to is one it is right not to fight: giving way to him is the way up.
- **The story forks** (`"if": [better, "up" | "down"]` on a step: walked only by the journey on that branch; `Betters.on_branch`, `Betters.skip`, asked in `Game.advance` and nowhere else). New step kind `"dare"` (`boss`: a bout with a better, done however it goes). **Give both branches steps, make the losing one the longer, and put what mends the wound on it**; a lord met twice (Pang Juan under the tree) is a `"dare"` and then, on the down branch only, a `"kill"` with a trial. `Game.reached` and `Game.done` are still plain comparisons of the index, so a `vis` that begins or ends on a step of one branch works for the other too. Never put a crossroads, a breakthrough or anything a later chapter's `vis` names on one branch only. The bot stands in front of them at once and loses (`--yield`: gives way; `--bot-watch`: as somebody who had watched each three times, and wins; `--bot-up` with `--bot-start`: begins on the up branch): **play all three from the chapter's first step before pushing**.
- **A level past the last realm has a name** (`Data.realm_name`, `level_name`: "Early Fourth Sky Above"): never index `Data.REALMS` with a foe's level.
- **The three people the story is about say I** (`scripts/memoir.gd`): a page (`PAGES`, in `ORDER`: `who` is `lin`, `yue` or `elder`; `after` a step; `if` a branch) is written once when its step is done (`Memoir.written`, from `Game.advance`), read out the next time he stands free (`Main._page_scene`) and again from Esc > In Their Own Words. **With no window, or under the bot, it is only written**, so the bot's journey is the same. Lin Feng says "I" and never what he is; Yue Ling writes *to* him ("you"); the Elder says "the child"; nobody who is dead by his hand writes. **A new chapter gets a page at its close** (after its breakthrough) by one of the three, and a fork gets a page for each side. A page changes no number and nothing may ask for one.

## Papers, the lists, the hammer, and a post that goes both ways
- **Every Dao has a paper** (`scripts/seals.gd`: the ids are those of `Rites.ORDER`): a road walked or a weapon at Adept is examined at a desk of the Hall of Seals (`Seals.DESKS`: offices that also keep the post; `Main.offer_seals`, "Not today" first; with a window what is shown to the desk is the Dao's rite) and sealed at the grade his standing has earned (`due`, `GRADES`), to be sat again when he has grown past it; a **forbidden** Dao is not certified but *abjured*, and that paper is **void from the day he sits to the art** (`valid`). **A new Dao needs a line in `Seals.ASKS`** (the tests count seventeen against `Rites.ORDER`). A paper changes one number (`Seals.mod`: a purse) and opens doors (the lists, an auction's sealed room); **nothing a paper opens may be something another chapter's step needs**, and a step that asks for papers (`"kind": "seal"`) asks for no more than six (one road, two weapons, the three of the shelf: what every journey can hold).
- **A meet is three bouts, alone** (`scripts/lists.gd`, `MEETS`, called at an office's counter: `Main.offer_lists`, `meet_bout`): the battle is started with `"meet"` in its context, the party is the hero only, and the rival is met at his own level on the day (`Lists.foe`, `ROUNDS`). **Losing a bout is the outcome `"out"`** (`Battle._out`, `Main._meet_done`): nobody is carried home, no death is counted, nothing is taken but the fee he paid; the meet is finished at the place that round is worth and a purse is paid. Keep it so, and keep a purse worth less than the road. A step of kind `"meet"` is done however the meet went. **A new rival needs** an enemy with a `person:` shape (in `UI.SHAPES`; the last of a meet is `"boss": true`), an entry in `Lists.RIVALS` (a `temper`, three `jeers`, a word for `won` and for `lost`), `"bout:<id>"` in `Data.DIALOGUE` and a story in `Lore.BEAST` or `Lore.BOSS`; a new meet's office must also be a desk (the bot sits for a missing paper at the same counter).
- **Words across a ring** (`Lists.WORDS`, `TEMPERS`; the fifth line of the battle menu in a bout, where "Step out" has taken the place of Flee, `Battle._say_word`): one a turn, each once a bout, and **saying it does not cost the turn**. `Lists.word` is pure: what a word does depends on the temper it is said to (a table of four tempers by three words: `atk`, `open`, `mine`, `crowd`, and the answer, with `%s` for the rival), and every number in it stays within the bounds the tests check. A new word needs a line in every temper and in `SAID`; a new temper a line for every word. A boast is settled when the bout ends (`Lists.settle`). What was said is remembered (`Game.said_to`) and may bring a letter (`"said": [rival, word]` on a letter of mail.gd). The bot says `Lists.best_word` once a bout; nothing a rival says may call the hero a man.
- **An auction room sells by the day, never by lot** (`scripts/auction.gd`, `HOUSES`, at an office's counter: `Main.offer_room`, `bid_on`; "Let them all go" and "Let it go" are first): four lots a day from the house's own catalogue, the fourth in a sealed room that three papers open and where the **curios** are (one of each, weightless, good for nothing). Three bidders who are there every day each have a ceiling for a lot (`Auction.ceiling`, a share of its value by the day); a call nobody will top wins and is what is paid. **Sell nothing there that can be sold on** (no goods of `Load.GOODS`, no wares: the tests check) and nothing with weight. A step of kind `"bid"` asks for one lot of a `house`; the bot takes `Auction.cheapest`. A new house needs an office, three bidders with a line each, a catalogue of things in `Auction.VALUE`, and two curios.
- **The post goes both ways** (`scripts/mail.gd`, the second half; `Main.offer_post`, "Not today" first): he writes to one of `PENPALS` and an answer is at any counter `ANSWER` days later (a new one each time, round again; nobody dead by his hand answers); a parcel of what is on his back goes home to the storehouse (`send_home`, by `Load.stowable`); a gift goes to the chest of a town he is not standing in (`remit`: the same gift as `Weal.give`, the same three a town); a sealed bid goes to tomorrow's sale in another town's room (`Auction.post_bid`, opened by `Auction.settle` the next time he asks at a counter) and what it wins waits as a parcel (`collect`). **None of it may be asked for by a step, a trial, a notice or an ending, nothing in it tips a scale, and the bot uses none of it** (the tests read its source).
- **A chapter with no lord is allowed** (Part XXXIX has none: its bouts are the lists'): it still needs a crossroads with marks, letters, fates and an echo, a page at its close, a realm, and bands in caves no chapter had used (frost, dunes and yanggu are taken now too).

## Leaves, several roads at once, and fuller towns
- **The card games are leaves** (`scripts/yezi.gd` rules, pure; `scripts/leaves.gd` the table, drawn; the class is `Yezi` because `Nature.Cards` is the grass): forty leaves, eight of each of the five phases, and two games: the **Round** (tricks: `Phases.QUELLS` takes it whatever is written, `Phases.FEEDS` where neither overcomes counts double, else the higher leaf; seven points) and **Three in the Morning** (seven leaves, three set down as the morning, the evening must be worth more; a morning taken is three acorns and an evening four). **Every deal is drawn from `Game.card_n` and who sits opposite**: nothing is thrown, and `Yezi.play_out` gives the tests the hand the screen shows. **Nothing is staked and nothing is ever paid to him**: the loser pours (`Yezi.tea`, to the house), so it is not a game of the mat and cannot be played for a living; keep it so (the tests play four hundred hands and read the source for a stone added). A master (`Yezi.MASTERS`: `at` an office's counter or somebody who stands in the land, a `game`, a `skill` of 0 to 2, a painted `leaf`, three lines) gives the leaf they painted once (`leaf_<id>` in `Data.ITEMS`, weightless; Esc > Leaves chooses which is brought: it takes the place of the least leaf he is dealt) and will not sit twice in a day. **A new master needs** all of that and an item; a new game needs an entry in `GAMES`, a way for each skill to play it, a `best` for the bot and a case in `leaves.gd`. "Not today" is the first answer wherever a table is offered, the old hands of the towns (the role `leafer`) play a hand with anybody, and the bot sits down only inside a step of kind `"cards"` (`of`: a master beaten; or `n` hands won since it began).
- **He may hold several roads at once, and each is harder for it** (`scripts/braid.gd`): `Game.dao` is the road he leads with (what the peoples, the rites and every older line read), `Game.braid` the others. A stele asks how a road pointed at is to be walked (`Main.offer_braid`: "Leave it" first, then "Walk it as well" or the old exchange). **All of the burden is four tables** (`GAIN`: hundredths of a battle's point each road learns, fractions in `Game.dao_part`; `SHARE`: how much of each passive he has; `QI`: what every technique costs; `REALM`: how many his realm lets him hold) and **the first entry of each is what one road always was**: `Braid.mod` returns the old number exactly for one road, the bot walks one road until a step of kind `"braid"` asks for two (`BRAID_PLAN`), and every older test is a one-road journey's. A new passive of a Dao goes in `Data.DAO_MODS` and is asked through `Game.dao_mod` with the value somebody with no road has as its fallback (1 for a multiplier, 0 for something added): that is what several roads are added up over. A trial that asks for a Dao (`["dao", id, tier]`) is met by any road he holds. Keep each table strictly harder with every road, and all of them together no better at learning than one (the tests check).
- **The towns are fuller, and nobody who lived there has changed** (`Folk.MORE`: town -> role -> how many have come since): they are made after everybody of `Folk.TOWNS` from a seed of their own (`folk2:<town>`), so every older name, body, day and errand is who it was and the Great People are still the last of a town's `roles`. **Add people to a town in `MORE`, never in `TOWNS.roles`**, and count a role with `Folk.of_role(town, role)` (an errand of a new chapter may be among them). Three kinds are new (`leafer`, `hawker`, `storyteller`): a new kind still needs what every role needs (a day in `ROLES`, places, bodies, a title, four lines, `Living.PAY` and `GOALS`, a kind in manners.gd).
- **Good deeds and bad ones are plights** (`scripts/plights.gd`): on about one day in four somebody grown of the towns is in the middle of something (`Plights.of`: read from their seed and the day, never thrown for, **never a child**, once a day a person), and a word with them offers three things (`Main.offer_plight`): **"Walk on" first**, the kind thing (`good`: costs stones by his realm or hours, and never pays) and the wrong thing (`bad`: pays a `gain` worth less than the road, or nothing). What a deed weighs is in three tables: `Creed.causes` (kindnesses and people wronged, each up to a measure), `Weal.ACTS.kind` / `wronged` (regard), and for a wrong with `"charge": true` the charge `Crime.CRIMES.wrong` (the one wronged is the witness). **A new plight needs** an entry in `PLIGHTS` and `ORDER` (the roles it happens to, what he sees as narration, and for each of the two things a label, what it means and what is said after: lines that fit the box and read of a hero of either sex), nothing else. No step, trial, notice, letter or ending may ask for a deed of either kind, and the bot walks on.
- **A chapter's cards, like its bouts, are its own lords**: Part XL has no lord and no trial; its foes are in three more caves (the Mulberry Silk Woods, Glowcap Vale, the Heights of the Magpie Bridge are taken now too), and somebody new who stands in a cave (`ju_gong`) is written `"late": true, "after": true, "cave": -1`.

## A sixth realm, sects, sides, raids and the roads' traffic
- **The world is six realms, and Part XLI is set in the sixth** (`Atlas.STRATA.lakes`: Yunmeng with Reedhaven, Cold Mountain with Clearbell, the Black Water with Ashmarket, the Wolf-Tooth Pass with Knifegate). The fifth is still the Far Countries, where nothing of the story is; the bot walks `REALMS_WALKED` (five: never `"far"`). A town down the river counts for nothing until he has stood in its country (`Weal.towns`), like a town only an ark reaches.
- **A way between realms that comes later in the story is `"late"`** (`Data.LADDERS` with `"late": "a"` or `"b"`: the river-boat of Longmen). `MapGen.ladder_ends` leaves that end out, so nothing of its region's own map is made for it; `Atlas._place_late_ladders` stands it **last of all**, on open ground near the tile written, open all round and three tiles from anything, and the other end comes out beside it (`Atlas.late_ladder_out`). `Roads._lay` ignores it. **Never write a new ladder end at a fixed tile of an older region**: dump both checkouts, and the only new line must be the landing.
- **In a realm made for its chapter, write things where they stand**: fixed tiles are fine there (nothing older to move), the hunt is for the region's own `pool` (the test of hunts allows it in `"lakes"`), and a region needs what any region needs (theme in `Look.THEMES` and `EXTRA`, `MapGen.DENSITY`, `Scatter.ECO`, `Props`, `Fauna`, `Caves.LOOK_OF`, `Music.OF_THEME`, a dress, a name in `Menu`, a word of Yue Ling's in `Bonds`, a town in `Data.TOWNS`, `Folk.TOWNS` and `MORE`, `Giants.TOWNS`, `Weal.TOWNS`, an office). `art/`-free: its people use the bodies of the towns.
- **A town is what its sect is** (`scripts/sides.gd`): `Sides.TOWNS` says which towns are upright and which crooked; every other town is plain and **nothing there may touch a plain town** (the tests walk all thirteen). What a side does goes through pure functions: `Sides.mod` (prices by the Way he walks), `lawless` (asked in `Crime.standing` and `watch_due`: nobody in a crooked town reads a bill), `purse_due` / `take_purse` (a cutpurse, once a day, by the day and the town, never from a guest of the sect, worth less than the road), `alms_due` / `take_alms`. A new side needs a line in every table (`NAMES`, `DESC`, `PRICE`, `LINES` of sixty-four letters, `WORD`) and a dress (`Arch.DRESS`). Nothing a side shuts or takes may be something a step needs.
- **Sects** (`scripts/sects.gd`): `SECTS` (his own first: it keeps no hall), each with three sayings of its own, a custom, how it pays, four rank names, a town, an office that keeps its hall and a master who stands in its country. **A new sect needs** all of that, a greeting for every rank (`GREET`), a keepsake (`TOKENS`, an item that weighs nothing), and **four tasks** in `TASKS` and `TASK_ORDER`: **the first two a hunt in its own country and a road kept (`["kills", ...]`, `["raids", n]`), neither foul**, since a step of kind `"sect"` asks for at most two and the bot takes them in order (`Sects.next`: never a foul one). A crooked sect has exactly one foul task (`"foul": true`: `["more", "deeds_bad", 1]`, and its `ask` says "foul"); an upright one has none. Each sect pays its own way in `Sects.pay` / `finish`; keep every task worth less than the road. No step names a task.
- **Raids** (`scripts/raids.gd`): from `Raids.OPEN` on, every watch of three hours a raid stands at the camp and on the high road of every region the journey has seen that has something wild of its own. **Nothing is placed and nothing is thrown for**: `sites` is read once from the camps and the roads, `on` from the day and the hour, `group` from the region's pool and the watch. A raid is broken by walking up to it (`World._raid_check`, `Main.start_raid`: fought, or scattered by his realm), and only then is anything written (`Game.raid_done`, `raids_broken`, `step_raids`, `Weal.ACTS.rescued`). **A raid nobody breaks costs no town anything**: keep it so. The raiders are no pack of the land (`"late"`, never `Almanac.put_down` or `Wilds.culled`). New step kind `"rescue"` (`n` raids broken since it began; `layer`: of that realm; ask for no more than three); the bot goes to `Raids.nearest`.
- **Traffic** (`scripts/traffic.gd` rules, `scripts/wagons.gd` models): the fleet of a realm is read from `Roads.of(layer).lines` (so many to a stretch, the kind from the stretch and its number); where a vehicle is, is the fleet and the clock (`Traffic.where`: pass **all the daylight there has been**, `Game.day * Clock.DAY + Game.clock`, since the clock of the day wraps). Day-vehicles stand at night; `"night": true` runs by `playtime`. A cart is held short of a raid on the road. **Drawn only** (`World._traffic_tick`, with a window, round where he is), stood on no tile, and the bot knows nothing of it. A new kind needs a line in `KINDS`, `ORDER` and `SAID`, a crew of bodies of the towns, and a model in `Wagons._body` facing +Z (or `"ride"`: one of the carriages); `"only": layer` keeps it to one realm, where it leads every stretch of the high road.
- **A journey dropped at a late step keeps what it had seen** (`Main.begin_at_sect` used to wipe `Game.seen` after `--bot-start` had filled it): anything that asks where he has been (raids, camps, coaches, the kite) can be tried from a late step.

## Hearths (the six cultures) and who the hero is
- The peoples of the land are the **Six Hearths** in `scripts/cultures.gd` (towns, keepers, greetings, tables, fires, customs, standing, the six beginnings and the five endings). A new town of the land belongs to a hearth (`towns`, `areas`); a new chapter's errands for a hearth carry `"hearth": "<id>"` on their steps, done in that hearth's own way, and an errand among its townsfolk names its `"town"`.
- The hero can be a man or a woman. **Write story text about "Lin Feng" and "he" as always**; `scripts/voice.gd` tells it of her on the way to the screen (narration by rule, speech only by what the hero is called). Where the rule gets a new line wrong, add the whole line to `Voice.FIX`; `tests/run_tests.gd` reads every conversation through it. Words about nobody but the hero (an outfit, a ride) go through `Voice.her`.
- New people need a Blender script in `art/characters/`; women are marked `"fem": True` there and, if they are named without a woman's title, listed in `Voice.WOMEN`.

## Performance
- `godot --path . -- --bench=build/bench.json` (with `xvfb-run` on a server) times the opening of the world and counts what a frame draws; `--census` lists the triangles in the scene by kind (`--census-view`: only what the camera sees). Compare draw calls and primitives between two builds on the same machine; the frame rate of a software renderer says little.
- Keep the importer's LODs when a mesh is rebuilt (`Assets.lods_of`), give anything heavy that is scattered out to 100 m a far mesh (`Terrain3D.far_mesh`), and do not hand the renderer people or things further off than they can be made out (`World.ENT_SHOW`). `Data.step_index` is a lookup: `Game.reached` is asked thousands of times a frame.
